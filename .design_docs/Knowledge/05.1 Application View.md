
> **The product is not an AI procurement application. It is a Purchase Decision Workspace.**

The customer does not want agents, Graph RAG, knowledge graphs, document parsing, or even primarily a comparison matrix.

They want:

> **“I have a purchase to make. Help me get to a confident, defensible decision faster.”**

Everything else should disappear behind that experience.

---

I would define the product around this funnel:

```text
                PURCHASE REQUEST
                       │
                       ▼
              "What are we buying?"
                       │
                       ▼
              "What do we need?"
                       │
                       ▼
             "What did vendors offer?"
                       │
                       ▼
              "How do they differ?"
                       │
                       ▼
             "What are the risks?"
                       │
                       ▼
             "What should we choose?"
                       │
                       ▼
                HUMAN DECISION
                       │
                       ▼
             "Create the business
                deliverables"
```

So the application has one north-star metric:

## **Time to Confident Purchase Decision**

Not:

- number of agents
    
- number of documents processed
    
- number of graph nodes
    
- number of AI interactions
    

Those are implementation metrics.

# 5. The AI Intake experience

Once the project is created, don't immediately send them to a blank project dashboard.

Show:

# **AI is understanding your purchase**

```text
Analyzing your purchase...

✓ Business objective identified
✓ 18 requirements found
✓ 4 vendors identified
✓ 12 documents processed
✓ Budget information found

⚠ 3 items need clarification
```

Then the AI starts a guided interaction.

### Example

> **I understand this purchase is for enterprise laptops across four regions.**
> 
> I've identified 18 requirements and 4 vendors.
> 
> Before I compare the vendors, I need to clarify three things.

Then:

> **Should 4-hour incident response be mandatory?**
> 
> `Mandatory` | `Preferred`

That's a much better experience than a 25-field procurement form.

# 15. This is where I would put the CEO-level UX

The AI should tell the decision maker:

## “Here is what you need to know.”

For example:

```text
DECISION BRIEF

1. Vendor A is the strongest overall fit.
2. Vendor B is 9% cheaper.
3. Vendor B fails one mandatory SLA.
4. Vendor C has the strongest implementation capability.
5. No vendor has a material security gap.
6. Final decision depends primarily on
   cost vs SLA trade-off.
```

Then:

### **Decision required**

```text
○ Approve Vendor A

○ Request negotiation with Vendor B

○ Reopen evaluation

○ Continue analysis
```

That turns AI analysis into an actual workflow.

> “Ask anything”

> “What are Vendor B's weaknesses?”

> “Show me the high-impact risks.”

# 24. If I were CEO, I would define the product promise as

> ### **From procurement information overload to a decision-ready purchase.**

And internally I would establish five product principles:

### 1. **Zero-to-value quickly**

A user should be able to create a purchase and give the AI documents in minutes.

### 2. **AI does the work, human controls the decision**

AI prepares, analyzes, explains and recommends. The human approves.

### 3. **Every important conclusion is traceable**

No black-box vendor ranking.

### 4. **One purchase = one source of truth**

Chat, documents, requirements, vendors, evidence, comparison, decision and artifacts all belong to the same project.

### 5. **Business output over AI output**

The final success is not “AI generated a good answer.”

It is:

> **“The business was able to make and communicate a better purchasing decision.”**
