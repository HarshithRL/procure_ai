
> **Purpose:** Establish the generic procurement domain knowledge required by the Procure AI Workspace to understand vendor comparison before applying organization-specific rules.

## 1. What is Vendor Comparison?

Vendor comparison is the structured evaluation of multiple suppliers against a common purchase requirement, using comparable commercial, technical, operational, compliance, risk, and qualitative information.

The objective is not simply to identify the cheapest vendor.

The objective is to determine:

> **Which vendor provides the best overall fit for the defined business requirement, considering cost, scope, capability, risk, delivery, and long-term commercial impact.**

A vendor comparison therefore transforms:

```text
Business Requirement
        +
Vendor Information
        +
Evaluation Criteria
        +
Evidence
        ↓
Like-for-Like Comparison
        ↓
Scoring / Ranking
        ↓
Trade-offs & Risks
        ↓
Decision Support
```

## 4. Like-for-Like Comparison

The central principle of vendor comparison is **comparability**.

Different vendors may structure their proposals differently, use different terminology, bundle different services, or include/exclude different costs.

Therefore the system must normalize responses into a common comparison structure.

```text
Vendor A
"4-hour response"

Vendor B
"Critical response within 240 minutes"

Vendor C
"Response SLA: 0.167 days"

             ↓

Common Representation

SLA Response Time
= 4 hours
```

Comparability also requires recognizing that:

> **A lower quoted price does not necessarily mean a lower total cost.**

## 5. Commercial Comparison

Commercial evaluation may include:

- Initial purchase cost
    
- Recurring cost
    
- Implementation cost
    
- Internal effort
    
- Discounts
    
- Indexation
    
- Renewal
    
- Multi-year cost
    
- Volume-based pricing
    
- Optional services
    
- Additional costs
    
- Total Cost of Ownership
    

A generic TCO model is:

```text
TCO
=
Vendor Recurring Cost
+
Vendor One-Time Cost
+
Internal Cost
+
Other Applicable Cost
```

For multi-year purchasing:

```text
Year 1
Year 2
Year 3
...
Year N
    ↓
Total TCO
```

## 6. Scope Comparison

The AI must determine whether vendors are actually proposing the same scope.

Important questions:

- What is explicitly included?
    
- What is excluded?
    
- What is optional?
    
- What is dependent on assumptions?
    
- What remains undefined?
    
- Which mandatory requirements are not met?
    

A vendor can appear cheaper because it provides a narrower scope.

Therefore:

```text
Quoted Price
      ≠
Comparable Price
```

unless scope has first been normalized.

## 8. Comparison Result States

The AI should preserve the difference between explicit non-compliance and missing information.

```text
MEETS
PARTIAL
FAILS
NOT SPECIFIED
NOT APPLICABLE
CONFLICTING
```

For example:

```text
Requirement:
4-hour SLA

Vendor A → MEETS
Vendor B → FAILS
Vendor C → NOT SPECIFIED
```

`NOT SPECIFIED` should not automatically become `FAILS`.

---

## 9. Decision Intelligence

A comparison becomes useful when the system explains the **why** behind the result.

The AI should surface:

```text
Vendor
├── Strengths
├── Weaknesses
├── Requirement Gaps
├── Commercial Position
├── Risks
├── Assumptions
├── Trade-offs
├── Score
└── Ranking
```

The final objective is:

> **Evidence-backed decision support, not autonomous vendor selection.**
